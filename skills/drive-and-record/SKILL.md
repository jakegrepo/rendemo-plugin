# Drive it yourself, and record it

The user has asked for a demo of their product and they do NOT want to walk it themselves, and they
do NOT want to tell you which buttons to click. You drive their signed-in product tab — read each
page, decide the path, click and type — while the Rendemo recorder captures the whole thing. What
comes out is a real recording (real screens, real transitions, real data), and because it is a
recording there is no free-roam replica for anyone to wander into, which makes this the SAFE choice
for a product whose screens are full of other people's data — a CRM, an inbox, a leads list.

This is one rung of the capture ladder in `new-demo`. Reach for it when all three are true: the demo
is behind a login, the user wants the agent to do the walkthrough rather than record it themselves,
and you can drive their browser through the extension. If the user would rather click through it
themselves, that is a plain recording (`new-demo`), and it is better when they have it in them —
nobody knows the product's story better than they do.

## Ask first — two or three short questions, then get out of the way

You are about to navigate a product you have never seen toward a goal only the user knows. Ask
before you start, not after. Keep it to three, lead with your read so they can just say yes, and
never turn it into an interview:

1. **What is this demo for?** Selling to prospects, onboarding new users, teaching one feature. The
   answer sets the story shape and the theme, exactly as in `new-demo`'s intent table — the whole
   reason to ask is so the path you choose has a destination.
2. **What is the one outcome it should land on?** The screen or moment that makes the case — the
   populated dashboard, the sent campaign, the analytics that prove it works. This is the payoff you
   steer toward; everything you click is in service of reaching it.
3. **Anything I must not touch?** A real destructive control, a customer's private record, a live
   send. You already refuse logout/delete-shaped navigation and destructive-looking clicks, but the
   user knows their product's landmines and this is your chance to hear them.

Then start. Do not ask a fourth question, and do not ask them to plan the steps — the steps are
yours to find.

## Starting the session

`rendemo_start_drive_session({ url })` with the signed-in entry url. It returns a code. Hand the
user these steps verbatim — do not paraphrase:

1. Have the Rendemo Chrome extension installed and connected to your Rendemo account.
2. Open your product in Chrome and sign in the way you normally would.
3. Click the Rendemo extension icon and choose **Drive & record this tab**.
4. Paste the code, and approve the one-time permission prompt.
5. Keep that tab in front and tell me once you are connected.

Then wait for them to say they are connected. Your first `rendemo_drive` call also tells you whether
they are: a "session has not started" result means the code is not pasted yet — that is not a
stopping point, keep going.

## Navigate it yourself — the read, decide, act loop

This is the part the user is paying for, and the rule is simple: **you choose every target; you
never ask them which one.** Each `rendemo_drive` returns what the page looks like after the action,
including a `read` outline where every button, link and field carries a selector in `«…»`. That
outline is your map.

1. **Read the landing.** `rendemo_drive({ sessionId, url, action: { op: "read" } })`. Now you can see
   the product's real navigation and controls.
2. **Decide the next move toward the outcome** the user named. Pick a target from the outline by its
   selector, or by its visible text.
3. **Act.** `navigate` to a section, `click` a control, `type` into a field, `scroll` to reveal more.
   Pass the selector or the text; the extension resolves it and clicks the real element.
4. **Read again**, and repeat. Narrate what you are doing in a sentence per step, so the user
   watching the tab move understands the story you are building.

Discipline that makes the difference between a demo and a screen-recording of aimless clicking:

- **Follow one path to the payoff.** Five to eight meaningful screens. A demo that opens every menu
  is a sitemap, not a story.
- **Every move earns its place.** If a click does not carry the viewer toward the outcome, do not
  make it.
- **The replica is the real product.** Forms submit, filters filter, data is real. That is the point
  — but it also means you are acting in a live account, so stay on the path and off anything the user
  flagged.
- **Do not type secrets.** Typing into a password field is refused; do not try to route around it.

When the outline does not show a control you need — an app whose navigation is a canvas or a
JS-only widget with no link or role — say so plainly and ask the user to click that ONE step
themselves, then carry on. That is the known limit of driving through the page rather than the
debugger, not a failure to hide.

## Finishing, then the build

When the workflow has reached the outcome, `rendemo_finish_drive_session({ sessionId })`. That stops
the recorder; the take uploads and auto-directs itself, exactly like a hand recording. Then:

- `rendemo_watch_captures()` — no crawlId — holds open until the project lands and its story
  director finishes. If it says nothing yet, call it again; it is not a stopping point.
- `rendemo_get_plan` to read the directed draft, then the `new-demo` build pass: tighten the copy,
  set the theme and motion to match what the demo is for, write the title and end cards, review the
  frames, and publish once the user agrees. `demo-craft` governs the step scene.

The demo is a recording, so everything `new-demo` and `demo-craft` say about a recorded demo applies
from here — this skill's job ends when the take has landed.

## Waiting is a tool call, not a turn

`rendemo_drive` and `rendemo_watch_captures` hold the connection open and return when something
happens. When one comes back still-not-done, call it again immediately. Do not summarise the wait,
do not ask the user to check, and do not end your turn — nothing on the other side resumes it.

## Tools

`rendemo_start_drive_session` → (hand over the paste steps) → `rendemo_drive` (`read` → decide →
`navigate`/`click`/`type`/`scroll`, repeated) → `rendemo_finish_drive_session` →
`rendemo_watch_captures` → `rendemo_get_plan` → the `new-demo` build pass → `rendemo_publish_demo`.
